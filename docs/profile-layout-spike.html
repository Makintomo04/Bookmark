Where things sit on your profile

An audit of the owner's profile as it stands on main, and four layouts that move the smaller sections to the side. Each one is drawn at desktop width with real section shapes, then the order a phone would get. A five-reader panel then stress-tested them and agreed on one: B+, a reading-first page in bands. A refined D then took B+ to a tie on points, and B+ won the vote 3 to 2. Built, B+ didn't hold up, so a UX-only review with a senior product designer led to Iteration 2, the current design.

Today

One long column, with one good idea in it

Below the header, everything takes the full width except Favourites and Lists, which share a row from lg up. That pairing is the strongest part of the page. The rest stacks in this order: follow requests, genre picks for new shelves, Favourites | Lists, the shelf (Currently reading, Paused, Want to read, Finished, Did not finish, Not interested), Up next in your series, your reviews, the "make a list" prompt, then the authors you follow.

1

The goal row spends a full width on two lines of text

A 36px ring and "41 of 52 books in 2026 · 3 behind pace" sit on the left; the month and pages bars sit 208px wide on the right. At 1200px there's around 600px of nothing between them. It also reads as part of the header, yet only you see it: visitors get no goal at all.

2

The pairing only happens when you have both

With favourites and no lists (or the reverse), the one you have takes the full row. Favourites then become eight small covers across; on a test account with one list and no favourites, a single list card sits alone at the left of a 1,100px row. The page also has a different shape from a friend's. The side-by-side idea is right; it just isn't the page's structure yet.

3

Everything after the shelf is out of sight

Series, reviews and followed authors come after every shelf, each previewing two rows. With five shelves that's several screens down. Up next in your series is an action ("start book 3"), so it belongs near Currently reading, not under Not interested.

4

Small sections are given big rows

Followed authors are a handful of pills; achievements are only a stat in the header. Both are classic side-column content: short, glanceable, and not what you came to the page to do.

5

Owner and visitor pages have drifted

OwnerProfile and PublicProfile both pair favourites with lists, but the visitor page has no goal or series, and puts reviews inside the shelf block. Whatever layout we pick should be one skeleton with owner-only slots, so the two can't drift again.

6

The phone constraint is real, and the code protects it

The header comment asks that the first shelf cover be in view without scrolling on a phone. Any side column has to drop below the shelf on a phone (or slim down above it), never push the shelf down.

Four layouts

From the least change to the most

All four keep the banner, avatar and header buttons as they are. The sample reader is 41 books into a 2026 goal of 52, with two in progress, favourites, three lists and five followed authors.

A · Goal in the header

Smallest change

No side column. The goal leaves its own row and becomes a compact card on the right of the header, level with your name and stats. The page keeps one column below, so every shelf stays six covers wide.

Works because

  • Removes the emptiest row on the page for very little code: ReadingGoalWidget moves from children to a new header slot.
  • The goal reads as a fact about you, like your stats.

Falls short because

  • Doesn't touch findings 2 to 4: the page is still one long column.
  • The header gets busier; a long bio will meet the goal card.

B · Paired bands

Recommended

Your Favourites | Lists row, made the rule. The page is a stack of bands, and each band pairs a wide section with a narrow one, two thirds and one third. Currently reading sits beside the goal; Favourites beside Lists; the shelves keep the full width; and at the end, your reviews sit beside the authors you follow and your achievements.

Works because

  • Every side column ends where its band ends, so there's never a tall empty strip beside the shelves.
  • Shelves stay six covers wide, which is the core of the page.
  • The goal sits next to the books that move it.
  • Series moves up the page, next to what you're reading.
  • One Band component (wide + narrow, stacks below lg) serves owner and visitor pages.

Falls short because

  • Each band needs a rule for when one side is empty (no goal set, no lists); the wide side takes the row.
  • Reviews still come after every shelf, so on a big library they stay out of sight.
  • Medium effort: OwnerProfile and PublicProfile both get re-ordered.

C · Right-hand rail

Closest to Goodreads

A two-column page under the header: the main column (about 720px) holds Currently reading, the shelves and your reviews; a 300px rail holds the goal, favourites as a 3-across grid, lists, series, followed authors and recent achievements. The rail sticks while the shelves scroll.

Works because

  • Everything small lives in one predictable place, and it's all in view at the top.
  • The most "profile at a glance" of the four; readers coming from Goodreads will know it.

Falls short because

  • The rail runs out after about one screen, leaving a tall blank strip beside the shelves (or a sticky rail that hides content until you scroll back up).
  • Shelves drop from six covers to four, so each shelf preview shows a third fewer books.
  • Favourites, the most personal section, shrink into the margin.

D · Identity column

Biggest rejig

The header stops being a block on top. Under a shorter banner, a left column holds who you are: avatar, name, bio, stats as a list, the goal, followed authors and achievements. Everything to the right is your books. It's the shape GitHub and Letterboxd's diary use.

Works because

  • Content starts much higher: the shelf begins beside your name, not under it.
  • A clear split between "you" and "your books" that suits a sticky column.

Falls short because

  • Rewrites ProfileHeader, which the visitor and restricted profiles share, and changes the banner's overlap.
  • Same blank-strip problem as C once the left column ends.
  • The identity column collapses back to today's header on a phone, so there are two header layouts to keep in step.

What they found wrong with B as drawn

Reviews still came after every shelf, although the text said otherwise (now corrected above). Visitors have no goal or series, so the first band was half empty for them. Lists were squeezed into a third. With real data the bands went uneven: Ada's series are taller than the goal card, and twenty author chips are taller than three reviews. Pulling Currently reading out of BookGrid would also strand the shelf search below two bands.

⇄

Two conflicts, and how they settled

Reviews above or below the shelves: above, 5 to 0. Ada gave way once shelf search moved into the first band. Now first or favourites first: Kofi, Dana, Ada and Sam wanted what you're reading first. Mei needed her favourites above the fold for visitors on a phone. They settled it like this: on a visitor's phone the Now band is a single line of text, and everywhere else it's the cover strip.

What each traded

Kofi and Dana gave up the achievements slot (it's still linked from the header stat). Mei gave up four lists on desktop. Ada dropped her "2026 in numbers" column. Sam agreed to keep "Pick your next read" owner-only and hideable. Kofi gave up a list of shared circles beside Now, and gets circle progress on the book itself instead.

UX-only review · 29 October

Judged on experience alone, the built B+ falls short

Once B+ was built, it didn't look right on desktop. A fresh panel (the same five readers plus a senior product designer who has worked at Netflix and Airbnb) judged every option on the experience alone, with build effort ruled out. They worked through seven tasks: update progress, find a book, read the goal, start the next book, show who you are, see a friend's reading, and browse shelves. B+ was judged from screenshots of the real build.

1

Updating progress got harder

All six reviewers said this. Today's card shows "Page 0 of 500" and a pencil. The compact strip shows a thin bar and "0%", with no visible control, so people guess where to tap. The one task everyone does most lost its button.

2

The bands don't pair, so the page has holes

The goal card props the header open with about 150px of black under the stats. The series column is twice the height of the reading cards, which leaves a hole under the toolbar. Lists is one card in a half-width grey box, and reviews have nothing beside them. The designer's verdict: "the pairs rarely pair".

3

Weight is in the wrong places

The shelf toolbar's 48px pills outweigh the books above them, and they control shelves three sections further down. Every series row has a full shelf button, and a Did-not-finish book appears under "Up next". "Pick your next read" is the loudest thing on the page. Cover sizes change from section to section, so no row sets the rhythm.

4

The goal contradicts itself, and the phone clips

"17 behind pace" sits in amber next to "Hit your 2 this month" in green. On a phone, the second reading card is cut off mid-card.

5

The page has two jobs

The designer's framing: for you it's a home screen (resume, log, decide). For a visitor it's an identity page (who this is, their taste, what they're reading). The library comes third for both.

Iteration 1 · the designer's synthesis

D+'s top, B+'s bands below, shelves as tabs

This is the only side-by-side pairing that always shows. You on the left; on the right, what you're reading, with a labelled Update button, and what's next. Below that, everything runs full width, and a band only pairs when both sides have content.

Continue card

A 2:3 cover at 80×120, the title, the author, "p. 212 of 500 · 42%", a 6px bar in your colour, and a labelled Update button. Update opens a popover on the card with the page field already focused, a pages/% toggle, Save and Finished. Enter saves, then an Undo toast appears: two clicks, no modal. On a phone it opens a bottom sheet with a number pad. The cover and title open the book. On a phone, cards stack at full width: two at most, then "+N more".

→

Up next

One row of small covers that replaces both the series column and "Pick your next read". Series books come first, captioned "Book 2 · Middle Earth". Up to three picks from Want to read follow, captioned "From Want to read", and only when you're behind pace or have no series next. Each cover has a one-tap Start reading, with no shelf dropdowns. Did-not-finish books never appear here.

◔

Goal wording

The ring is in your colour. The headline reads "4 of 12 · 2 behind", and the next step reads "Read 1 more this month to catch up". If catching up would take more than four a month, it reads "17 behind pace · Adjust goal" instead. Only one coloured word shows at a time: amber when behind, green only when on or ahead of pace. Month and pages are grey, and open on click.

⌕

Search and shelves

Shelf search sits in the top zone, visible on load, with "/" as a shortcut. Results open across the full width under the top zone, with chips to narrow them to one shelf. Shelves become tabs (Want to read · Reading · Read · DNF · All). Sort, filter and view are 36px ghost icons on the tab row, and Select goes into an overflow menu. The toolbar never appears away from its shelves.

✓

Visitor page

The same top zone, minus everything only you see. The goal shows only if you opt in. A reading line comes first: "Reading Fourth Wing · p. 212 of 500 · 62% · +2 more", or "Not reading anything right now". Favourites come next. On a phone: header, reading line, favourites, reviews, lists, shelves. Nothing only the owner sees appears, so it's ready to screenshot.

✕

What it deliberately doesn't do

Picks stay on the first screen, at under a third of their old size and folded into Up next. That's for Sam's next-read task, against Ada, Mei and Dana's wish to move them lower. On a visitor's page, favourites come second, after one reading line, which all five readers asked for first. And B+'s pairing of Currently reading with series is gone, because it caused the hole.

Iteration 2 · after the readers reacted

Every book on the go, logged the way you read it

Iteration 1 scored 39 out of 50: 8 each from Ada, Sam, Kofi and Mei, and 7 from Dana. They raised the same gaps. A third or fourth book on the go got hidden, and long titles clipped. The "+2" on a visitor's reading line didn't say what it opened. Audiobooks and Kindle books couldn't be logged in their own units. Reviews slipped back below the shelves. Iteration 2 is the designer's answer, with each reader's one change folded in.

How the conflicts settled

Reviews against lists: both sit above the shelves everywhere, so Dana's dealbreaker holds. On a visitor's phone, lists come before reviews, as Mei asked. Dana gave up lists sitting beside favourites, so lists run full width. Search: on desktop it stays in the top zone for Ada. On a phone it moves into the sticky shelf tab row for Sam, one tap away wherever you've scrolled. A magnifier on the Continue heading jumps to it.

◔

Update sheet units

Print books log pages. Ebooks log pages, % or Kindle location. Audiobooks log hours and minutes of the total, or %. Any book can switch to chapters. A circle's book defaults to chapters. The sheet remembers the last unit you used for each book.

✕

Still not met

The circle marker shows only to circle members, because anyone else would get a spoiler and a privacy leak. A public goal never says "behind". On a phone, the goal's two lines sit behind a chip in the stats row. With four or more books on the go, a short gap can open under the identity column; the designer chose that over cutting books. And when a reader has picked no genres, picks fall back to "Popular in your circles", which still needs a reader check.

Built and committed

The polish, the Update sheet, chapters and the public goal

✓

Polish

The phone's tab row fades at both edges and keeps the open tab clear of them. Up next titles get their two lines, with a shorter "Want to read" tag. A short list's covers sit in the middle of its card. The circle figure sits on the bar line, so it never wraps, and the visitor's phone bar gets its tick. The desktop favourites hint no longer says "Tap", and with no favourites it folds into the Lists heading. A creator-sized test account (eight favourites, fifteen lists) checks the visitor page.

↻

The Update sheet

Update opens a panel under the card on desktop and a sheet from the bottom on a phone. It logs in pages (or time for an audiobook), percent, chapters ("Ch 26 of 59") or Kindle location ("Loc 2,310 of 5,120"), opening on the unit you last used, with the field ready: type, then Enter. Undo is in the toast. Finished asks for a rating, then shows what's next with one-tap Start. Chapters and locations are stored as a percent, so stats, goals and circles work as before.

◔

Circle marker in chapters, and the public goal

When the book has chapters, a circle reads "circle at Ch 21", mapped across members' editions by how far through they are. It shows on your page and, pinned first, on a member's page for anyone in that circle. "Show my reading goal on my profile" (Settings, privacy) is off by default. Turned on, visitors see "8 of 12 books in 2026", with "3 ahead" in green when you are ahead; it never says behind.

What it takes

  • Split BookGrid so Currently reading and the shelf search can live in the Now band, with results rendered under it.
